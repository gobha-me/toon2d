Styles

Toon · Flat colour, bold outline, soft 2-band shade · play

Good for
The safe default: readable at 1x on a busy level, works for any tone.
  • Reads at small sizes; the outline separates her from the level
  • Cheapest art: flat fills, ~25 body parts plus ~15 face pieces
  • Parameter face reads well (brows, lids, mouth all visible)
  • Flat shapes show rigid part rotation the most, so it is the most puppet-prone look
  • Looks generic Flash-era unless the art direction is strong
Cost
Art: 1-2 artist-days per character sheet (estimate). Rig: 20 bones, 3 spring chains, 22 face params.
Render: 3.5 ms/frame, headless software canvas, scene included (relative only; original cutout 1.6 ms)
Metroidvania: Yes. Solid default.
Tower climber: OK. Fine if the camera keeps her at 1/5 screen height or more.
How to
  1. Draw parts in a neutral pose on separate layers, overlapping at the joints
  2. Make each limb one mesh weighted to 2 bones, not two sprites
  3. Bones, rest pose, then clips: idle, run, jump, fall, land
  4. Layer procedural motion on top (springs, squash, idle noise)
  5. Outline: stroke-behind or an outline shader

Chibi / SD · 2.5 heads tall, stubby limbs, rubbery squash · play

Good for
Cute or comedic games, mobile, and tower climbers where the character is small on screen.
  • Short limbs hide joint problems
  • The big head means the face carries emotion even at tiny sizes
  • Squash and stretch reads as charm, not error
  • Short limbs limit poses: no reach and weak attack arcs
  • The head covers the body, so the bangs clipped the eyes in review
  • The head bob reads heavy at run speed
Cost
Art: cheapest, with fewer limb details. Rig: the same 20 bones with scaled proportions.
Render: 3.8 ms/frame, headless software canvas, scene included (relative only; original cutout 1.6 ms)
Metroidvania: Maybe. It fits the tone, but combat poses suffer.
Tower climber: Best fit. It reads when small and the vertical camera loves a big head.
How to
  1. Scale bone lengths, not the art: head x1.3, legs x0.72
  2. Push squash to 1.5-2x the toon value
  3. Enlarge the eyes about 1.3x and keep the brows above the bangs
  4. Shorten the run cycle stride so the feet don't skate

Cel anime · Thin coloured line, hard 2-tone shadow, rim light · play

Good for
Story-heavy games where gameplay must match cutscenes or key art.
  • The hard shadow plus rim light gives volume
  • The most anime look
  • Best face close-ups
  • Long thin limbs read weak at game scale
  • Hard shadows slide across rotating parts, a fresh puppet tell
  • More palette and shadow-shape work per character
Cost
Art: +30-50% for shadow shapes per part (estimate). Rig: the same.
Render: 3.7 ms/frame, headless software canvas, scene included (relative only; original cutout 1.6 ms)
Metroidvania: Yes, with a close camera (the character at 1/5 screen height or more).
Tower climber: No. Too thin when small.
How to
  1. Bake the shadow shape per part, or use a 2-band ramp shader with the light in screen space
  2. Rim light: an offset mask on the light side
  3. Thicken the far-side line so the limbs survive at 1x

Ink sketch · Boiling brush line, hatching, one spot colour, on 2s · play

Good for
Art-house, horror, short narrative platformers.
  • Line boil plus 2s timing hides the rig best: the lowest puppet read in review
  • Forgiving of rough art
  • Distinctive at a glance
  • Low contrast on paper, so gameplay readability suffers
  • Boil is busy to look at for long sessions; offer a setting
  • Hard to read hit states
Cost
Art: low. Runtime: highest of the vector styles (multi-pass lines).
Render: 3.8 ms/frame, headless software canvas, scene included (relative only; original cutout 1.6 ms)
Metroidvania: Risky for combat-heavy games.
Tower climber: No. Readability.
How to
  1. Redraw lines with per-tick jitter at 8 fps (boil), not every frame
  2. 2 passes, slightly off-register
  3. Keep one saturated spot colour for the player and threats

Pixel art · Rig drawn into a 1/4-res buffer, quantised, NN upscale · play

Good for
Retro Metroidvania and tower climbers: the strongest genre fit.
  • The low-res buffer kills the puppet read: rotations snap to the grid
  • Held 12 fps poses read as hand-made sprite animation
  • A small palette keeps it cohesive
  • Faces are 2 px eyes: no brows, a hand-placed pixel face per expression
  • Thin parts crawl and jitter as they rotate
  • CPU readback here; in an engine do it in a shader
Cost
Art: same parts plus a pixel face sheet. Runtime: in an engine the buffer is a shader, nearly free.
Render: 3.7 ms/frame, headless software canvas, scene included (relative only; original cutout 1.6 ms)
Metroidvania: Yes. Best fit.
Tower climber: Yes.
How to
  1. Render the character to a 1/4-res offscreen buffer
  2. Quantise to a fixed palette and add a 1 px outline pass
  3. Draw the face by hand in buffer pixels, driven by the same params
  4. Integer upscale with nearest neighbour, never fractional
  5. Hold poses on 2s; keep the root and camera smooth

Techniques, ON vs OFF

Left is OFF and right is ON, same inputs. Some clips play at half speed (marked).

Low-res pixel buffer vs full-res rig · visible gain 5/5 · play OFF

Fixes
The smooth vector-tween look, and mixels (rotated pixel art at the wrong resolution).
How
Render the character to a 1/4-res buffer, quantise, outline, then integer-upscale with nearest. Godot: a SubViewport at 1/4 size with nearest filter and the 2D snap-to-pixel setting, plus palette and outline in a CanvasItem shader.
When not to
With HD art. With non-integer window scales (use letterboxing). On thin parts under 2 px (they flicker).

Spring secondary (hair, scarf) · visible gain 5/5 · play OFF

Fixes
Hair and cloth glued on like a helmet. Starts and stops with no follow-through.
How
A short verlet chain per strand. It pulls toward a rest direction and inherits body velocity, with gravity plus a little flutter. Godot: Skeleton2D with SkeletonModification2DJiggle, or about 30 lines of verlet in _physics_process.
When not to
When it covers a hitbox or the face. On long chains that clip through the body. At pixel scale without snapping.

Easing, anticipation, overshoot · visible gain 4/5 · play OFF

Fixes
Robotic linear motion and weightless landings.
How
Cubic or Catmull-Rom keys. Crouch while charging (anticipation), stretch on take-off, and a damped squash spring on landing. Overlap springs on head and arms overshoot on stops. Godot: AnimationPlayer cubic interpolation, Tween TRANS_BACK or TRANS_ELASTIC, and a spring in _physics_process.
When not to
Never delay control after the take-off crouch: a short 4–6 tick squat plays while the jump is buffered, then the impulse fires; a tap (release before impulse) gets a short hop, a hold gets full height. Don't overdo the overshoot on small UI-like moves.

Animating on 1s vs 2s vs 3s · visible gain 4/5 · play OFF

Fixes
Too-smooth motion that reads as a vector tween.
How
Sample the pose at 12 fps (2s) or 8 fps (3s) and hold it. Keep the root, camera and physics smooth, or it judders. Godot: advance the AnimationTree with a quantised time, or seek on a timer.
When not to
3s on a fast run reads choppy (seen here). Never quantise the camera or the root motion. Dodges and attacks may need 1s or smears.

Continuous joint bends vs hinges · visible gain 3/5 · play OFF

Fixes
Pivot-ball elbows and knees, the paper-doll seam.
How
One outline per limb from root to tip, with an inner crease and an outer arc at the joint. Equivalent: one mesh weighted to two bones. Godot: Polygon2D with Skeleton2D bone weights.
When not to
On chibi or pixel, where limbs are too short to see the joint. On frame-swapped sprite limbs.

Idle noise, contrapposto, breathing · visible gain 3/5 · play OFF

Fixes
The statue idle and in-sync, mirror-image limbs.
How
Add per-bone value noise at different rates, a hip shift with counter-tilted shoulders, and a breath layer that speeds up with exertion. Godot: an additive AnimationTree layer, or procedural offsets on Bone2D.
When not to
Too much amplitude reads as jitter. Turn it off during precise aiming or dialogue close-ups.

Head-leads turn vs instant flip (half speed) · visible gain 2/5 · play OFF

Fixes
The instant mirror flip.
How
On a direction change, squeeze the body width for ~0.1 s, yaw the face toward the camera, then flip. Physics flips at once. Better: 2-3 drawn turn frames (a front view). Godot: a turn state in the AnimationTree that never blocks input.
When not to
Never delay the gameplay turn. With only one side view of the parts, expect a squeeze, not a turn (seen here).

Smears (half speed) · visible gain 1/5 · play OFF

Fixes
Strobing when a limb jumps far between held poses.
How
A 1-frame polygon from the previous to the current hand or foot position, filled in the limb colour, or drawn as dry-brush lines in ink. Godot: a Polygon2D toggled for one frame, or a shader on a motion trail.
When not to
On run cycles (near-invisible here). Use it on attacks, dashes and whips, and only when on 2s.
